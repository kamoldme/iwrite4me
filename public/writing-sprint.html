A 30-minute writing sprint: make space for the next sentence.

A 30-minute writing sprint can help you start a scene, make progress on a draft, or simply practice showing up. Use a notebook, your favorite editor, or iWrite.

Try the writing room

On desktop: try a 30-minute session without an account, once per network every seven days. Sign in or register to save your writing on the same browser.

Run a sprint in 30 minutes

This format works in person or online. Invite people to bring a draft or a blank page. Sharing their writing is always optional.

  1. Welcome · 3 minutes“Today is for drafting, not polishing. Choose your own project or one of the prompts below.”
  2. Choose a direction · 2 minutesPick one small goal: a scene, a paragraph, or a question to explore. Mute notifications if you can.
  3. Write · 20 minutesSet a shared timer. Leave conversation and feedback until the end. If you get stuck, write a question about what happens next and answer it without editing.
  4. Reflect · 5 minutesAsk: “What surprised you? What is one small next step?” People can pass, or share a thought without revealing their draft.

To write for the full 30 minutes, hold the welcome before the timer and reflect afterward. There is no word-count minimum.

Three prompts to get started

  1. A character finds a note addressed to them in a place they have never visited. It mentions a promise they do not remember making.
  2. Two people want the same object for entirely different reasons. Neither states their reason aloud.
  3. Describe a familiar room after one small thing has changed. Let the change reveal something about the person noticing it.

Make a focused writing space

Choose one draft and one small intention before you start. Put notifications away if possible, close tabs you do not need, and decide whether silence or background music helps you settle. If you pause to research a detail, leave yourself a note and come back to the draft.

Writing with a group? Agree on the length of the quiet writing period first. Participants can use their own notebooks or editors, and nobody needs to share what they have written.

Keep the habit going

Before leaving, choose a time for the next session. Keep the goal small enough to begin, and ask your group what would make the next sprint easier. Ask permission before sharing anyone’s feedback publicly.

Using iWrite for the sprint
